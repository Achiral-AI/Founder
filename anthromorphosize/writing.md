# Writing: blog posts, essays, concept pages

The bar: the news, engineering and research-explainer writing of Anthropic and
OpenAI. Their pieces state the point at once, attach conditions to every
number, show the trade-offs that cut against them, and end with somewhere to
go.

The voice stays the author's. These companies write as a company "we"; a
founder's post keeps the founder's "I", rhythm and edge. Meet their bar for
structure, evidence and finish, not their tone. Where a check here and the
author's established voice disagree, the voice wins and the check becomes a
note.

## What this writing does

- **The first paragraph carries the point.** A launch says what is new; an
  argument states its thesis; an explainer states its question or finding. Two
  or three short paragraphs at most come before the first heading.
- **Setup is disclosed up front.** If a result depends on a contrived or
  simulated scenario, the piece says so before the result.
- **Terms are pinned early.** A term the piece depends on is defined at first
  use, then used the same way throughout.
- **Depth is layered.** The plain idea or an analogy first, then the mechanism,
  then a link for readers who want the detail.
- **Headings say what the section covers**, in sentence case, consistently.
  Long pieces may number their sections and carry a contents list.
- **Prose carries the argument.** Lists are for items that really are separate;
  parallel items share one template.
- **Every number has its conditions**: what was measured, on which set or
  sample, with what settings, against what baseline. Methodology that changes
  how a number reads is in the piece, in a note or a methods section, not only
  in a linked paper. Incomparable numbers are flagged by the writer, not left
  for the reader to catch.
- **Trade-offs that cut against the author are shown.** If a result is mixed,
  the piece shows the mixed part.
- **Experience is labelled as experience.** "In our use" or "we observed" marks
  a claim that was seen, not measured.
- **Limits are stated.** At least one explicit limitation, or a sentence on what
  the result does not show. Mistakes are owned plainly, with the cause and the
  fix.
- **Superlatives are earned.** A "best", "first" or "fastest" sits next to the
  measurement that backs it, or it goes.
- **Hedges are specific.** Each hedge attaches to one claim and matches the
  evidence. No blanket hedging, and no unhedged extrapolation.
- **Sources are linked inline** with anchor text that says what the link is,
  plus the full paper or report where one exists.
- **Other companies are named exactly and described neutrally**, or not named.
  Nothing is dismissed without evidence.
- **Figures teach.** A caption says how to read the figure: what is plotted,
  the encoding, the sample and the conditions. Every image has alt text that
  describes it.
- **The ending gives the reader a next step**: a link, availability, a
  follow-up, an open question, or a way to get in touch.
- **The surface is clean.** A date and an author on every piece. One dash style,
  the serial comma, digits for data, units stated. A piece that has gone stale
  says so at the top and links what replaced it.

## Concept and explainer pages

Concept pages answer one question. On top of the checks below, they define the
term in the first sentence, stay narrow (one term or one distinction), show a
concrete example, and link to the longer guide or paper rather than becoming
one.

## Checks

1. **B** The first paragraph states the main claim, news or question.
2. **P** No more than about three short paragraphs before the first heading.
3. **P** Headings are sentence case, consistent, and each says what its section covers.
4. **B** Every number names what was measured, on what, under which conditions, and against what baseline.
5. **B** Nothing incomparable is compared without saying so.
6. **P** Methodology that changes how a number reads is in the piece, not only in a linked source.
7. **B** The piece states at least one limitation or what the result does not show, whenever it makes an empirical claim.
8. **B** Every superlative is backed by a measurement in the same paragraph, or cut.
9. **P** Claims from experience are labelled as such.
10. **P** Each hedge attaches to a specific claim and matches the evidence.
11. **B** Every external fact, paper or prior post is linked inline with descriptive anchor text (never "here").
12. **P** Every technical term or acronym is defined at first use and then used consistently.
13. **P** The plain idea comes before the mechanism, with a link for more depth.
14. **P** Lists hold only separate items; the argument stays in prose; parallel items share one template.
15. **B** Every image has descriptive alt text; every chart or diagram has a caption that says how to read it.
16. **B** Other companies and tools are named exactly and described neutrally, with evidence for any criticism.
17. **P** The ending gives the reader a concrete next step.
18. **P** The page shows a date and an author; typography is consistent (one dash style, serial comma, digits for data, units stated).
19. **B** On concept pages, the first sentence defines the term and the page stays on one term or distinction.
