# Plain-Language

Renders accessibility without dumbing down the underlying idea.

## Use When

- The audience is broad or mixed-expertise.
- Jargon blocks understanding.
- The author wants simpler language without losing precision.

## Edit Moves

- Replace specialized terms with plain equivalents where precision survives.
- Define unavoidable technical terms in context.
- Use familiar sentence structures.
- Prefer concrete examples over abstract explanation.
- Remove institutional, legalistic, or academic fog.
- Keep complexity in the idea, not in the phrasing.

## Questions

- What does the reader actually need this term to mean?
- Can the sentence be understood without prior insider knowledge?
- What simplification would become inaccurate?

## Stop When

A non-specialist can follow the idea and a specialist would not object to the accuracy.
