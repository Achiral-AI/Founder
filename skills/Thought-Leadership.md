# Thought-Leadership

Renders an original point of view rather than a competent summary.

## Use When

- The draft explains a topic but does not yet take a position.
- The author wants differentiated perspective, not generic expertise.
- The piece needs a sharper thesis, frame, or intellectual stance.

## Edit Moves

- Turn observations into claims.
- Name the tension others are missing.
- Replace consensus language with a defensible point of view.
- Add stakes: why this matters now, to whom, and what changes if it is true.
- Separate the author's insight from existing discourse.
- Remove performative contrarianism that is not supported by reasoning.

## Questions

- What would a smart reader not have said this way already?
- What is the author's earned disagreement?
- What frame makes the familiar material newly legible?

## Stop When

The piece advances a recognizable position that could only come from this author thinking seriously about this subject.
