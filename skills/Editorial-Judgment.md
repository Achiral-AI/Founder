# Editorial-Judgment

Renders writing that knows what to include, what to omit, what to emphasize, and where to stop.

## Use When

- The draft has good material but weak selection or order.
- The piece lacks a clear center of gravity.
- The author needs help deciding what the piece is really about.

## Edit Moves

- Identify the strongest idea and make the draft serve it.
- Move background below the claim unless context must come first.
- Cut sections that are true but not necessary.
- Give more space to the highest-value insight.
- Remove tangents, caveats, and examples that dilute the central argument.
- End when the reader has the intended conclusion, not when every related thought has been exhausted.

## Questions

- What is the one thing this piece must make impossible to miss?
- What is merely interesting?
- What should the reader remember or do afterward?

## Stop When

The piece feels selected, shaped, and finished rather than merely improved line by line.
