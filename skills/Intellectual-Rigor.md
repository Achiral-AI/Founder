# Intellectual-Rigor

Renders claims that are appropriately scoped, logically defensible, evidence-conscious, and explicit about uncertainty.

## Use When

- The draft makes claims that need stronger reasoning or evidence.
- The piece risks overclaiming.
- The author wants sharper thinking, not just sharper prose.

## Edit Moves

- Identify the claim, evidence, assumption, and inference.
- Scope conclusions to the available support.
- Separate examples from proof.
- Surface hidden assumptions when they affect the argument.
- Add uncertainty where the argument depends on incomplete information.
- Remove claims that are rhetorically useful but logically weak.

## Questions

- What would have to be true for this claim to hold?
- What evidence is being used, and what does it actually show?
- Where could a strong critic fairly object?

## Stop When

The argument can survive skeptical reading without pretending to prove more than it does.
