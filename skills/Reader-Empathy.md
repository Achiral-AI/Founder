# Reader-Empathy

Renders writing around the reader's existing knowledge, questions, objections, motivations, and cognitive load.

## Use When

- The draft is author-centered rather than reader-centered.
- The audience may be skeptical, busy, unfamiliar, or emotionally invested.
- The piece needs better sequencing around reader needs.

## Edit Moves

- Identify what the reader knows before arriving.
- Answer the question the reader is likely asking now.
- Introduce concepts in the order the reader needs them.
- Respect objections instead of brushing past them.
- Reduce cognitive load by grouping related ideas.
- Make relevance explicit when it is not obvious.

## Questions

- Who is reading, and what do they already believe?
- Where will they pause, resist, or get lost?
- What do they need before they can care?

## Stop When

The piece feels designed for the reader's path through the idea, not just the author's need to express it.
