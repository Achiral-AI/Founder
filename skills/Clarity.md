# Clarity

Renders ideas immediately understandable without sacrificing substance.

## Use When

- The reader may struggle to know what is being claimed.
- Sentences are correct but cognitively expensive.
- The draft contains implicit context, buried subjects, or delayed meaning.

## Edit Moves

- Put the main claim before supporting nuance.
- Name the subject, action, and consequence plainly.
- Split sentences when one sentence carries multiple operations.
- Replace abstract containers with the thing being discussed.
- Turn implied relationships into explicit ones.
- Use examples only when they clarify a real ambiguity.

## Questions

- What does the reader need to understand first?
- Which sentence carries the actual point?
- Where is the reader forced to infer a missing step?

## Stop When

A serious reader can understand the idea on first pass and still find the substance intact on second pass.
