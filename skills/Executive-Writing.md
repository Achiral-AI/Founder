# Executive-Writing

Renders brevity, strategic relevance, decisiveness, and information hierarchy.

## Use When

- The reader is a busy decision-maker.
- The draft needs to support judgment, alignment, or action.
- The material must distinguish signal from detail.

## Edit Moves

- Put the decision, recommendation, or status first.
- Separate what matters now from background.
- Make tradeoffs, risks, and next steps explicit.
- Use concise headings that reveal the point.
- Remove process detail unless it changes the decision.
- Convert vague updates into accountable facts and implications.

## Questions

- What decision or understanding does the reader need?
- What changed, what matters, and what should happen next?
- Which details belong in an appendix or follow-up?

## Stop When

A decision-maker can grasp the point, risk, and action quickly without losing strategic context.
