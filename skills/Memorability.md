# Memorability

Renders ideas that stick through strong formulations, contrast, rhythm, concrete imagery, and appropriately surprising language.

## Use When

- The draft is clear but forgettable.
- The piece needs lines readers can remember, repeat, or quote.
- The central idea deserves stronger phrasing.

## Edit Moves

- Sharpen the central claim into a durable sentence.
- Use contrast to make the distinction easier to retain.
- Replace abstract summaries with concrete images when apt.
- Vary sentence rhythm around key moments.
- Create a phrase that names the idea without becoming gimmicky.
- Remove cleverness that distracts from the point.

## Questions

- What should the reader remember tomorrow?
- Which sentence carries the most repeatable formulation?
- What image, contrast, or rhythm makes the idea easier to hold?

## Stop When

The piece leaves behind at least one precise, memorable formulation that serves the argument.
